# Story and voice

## Build an argument or dramatic movement

Make each scene answer the previous one and create pressure for the next. A documentary may move through claim, evidence, contradiction, consequence, and unresolved question. A poetic film may move through sensation, recognition, fracture, transformation, and return. Neither should follow the source's headings mechanically.

For explanatory work, a useful causal chain is:

`human tension → observed behavior or language → evidence → interpretation → decision → consequence → responsibility`

Use only the links the source supports.

## Narration

- Write for the ear: concrete nouns, short clauses, varied cadence.
- Let one sentence do one conceptual job.
- Put dates, names, and quotations where listeners can retain them.
- Use philosophical language as a turn in the argument, not as ornament.
- Match certainty to the evidence ledger.
- Give authentic audio and important documents room to speak.
- Avoid generic verdicts, empty oppositions, recap padding, and synthetic-sounding slogans.
- In Chinese, do not default to “不是……而是……”, “真正的……从来不是……”, “这不仅……更……”, stacked three-part judgments, or repeated thesis restatements. Use direct claims, source-native details, uneven human cadence, and scene-specific verbs.

Target occupancy is contextual:

- documentary or explainer: often 68–82%;
- poetic essay or brand film: often 50–72%;
- interview-led film: count authentic speech, not only narrator time.

These ranges diagnose density; they do not prescribe it. Long silence needs a visual or emotional reason.

## Dialogue and quoted speech

Keep character dialogue ordinary and role-specific. Do not make every speaker solemn, fluent, or perfectly structured. Preserve the language and cadence of authentic clips. If a translation appears as subtitles, keep the original audio unless the user approves dubbing.

Verify every direct quotation. When exact wording is unavailable, write a clearly framed paraphrase or original narration.

## Voice casting

- Narrator: choose register, age impression, pace, intimacy, and regional neutrality from the film's needs.
- Secondary roles: differ in rhythm and placement, not merely pitch.
- Do not cast every documentary in a deep, slow, grave voice.
- Generate a 10–20 second test containing names, numbers, questions, and sentence endings.
- Use punctuation for breath; avoid excessive ellipses or theatrical pauses.
- Default to a generic or newly designed synthetic voice.

## Audio lock

Final shot timing and subtitle cues follow generated audio, not the other way around. After synthesis:

1. transcribe every segment;
2. compare intended and spoken text;
3. listen to proper names, numbers, foreign terms, and sentence boundaries;
4. regenerate failures;
5. write measured duration and file path back to the plan;
6. retime the shot list;
7. create subtitle cues from word timestamps;
8. lock audio before picture lock.

## Credits

Represent visual and spoken credits independently. Never infer that a visible organization name should be narrated.
