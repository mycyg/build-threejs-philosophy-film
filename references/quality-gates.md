# Quality gates

## Plan gate

- Sources and embedded instructions are distinguished.
- Material claims have evidence IDs and locators.
- Direct quotations are verified or removed.
- Directions differ in thesis, evidence, shot grammar, and renderer—not only color.
- Storyboard includes the full narration, shot actions, source risks, asset counts, and renderer mix.
- Storyboard approval matches the current revision.

## Asset gate

- Every designed shot meets its candidate and selected minimums.
- Important shots have at least one viable alternate.
- Recurring subjects and motifs maintain continuity.
- Generated reconstructions cannot be mistaken for archives.
- Source media records provenance, rights notes, and exact locator.
- Authentic clips pass speaker, audio, transcript, context, and time-range verification.
- Project-bound generated images are stored in the project, not only in a cache.

## Audio gate

- Every narration item has a nonempty final audio file and measured duration.
- Full ASR audit covers every segment.
- Intended and spoken content match; failed segments were regenerated.
- Proper names, numbers, foreign terms, and endings were listened to manually.
- Subtitle cues come from final audio word timestamps.
- Authentic source speech keeps the correct speaker and language.
- Music ducking and source-audio transitions are smooth.

## Visual and edit gate

- Renderer choice follows shot function.
- Three.js contributes depth, persistence, procedural behavior, or transformation when used.
- No universal card, portal, particle, centered title, or template palette survives by default.
- Each long shot contains meaningful internal visual or information change.
- Declared still holds are intentional and editorially justified.
- Documents, interfaces, and captions remain legible at delivery resolution.
- Transitions preserve causality, direction, object, sound, or idea.
- Exact boundary and subtitle-change frames remain clean.

## Technical gate

- Staged validator passes for plan, assets, audio, and rendered output.
- All declared assets, voice files, source clips, music, and output files exist.
- Scene timeline covers the declared duration without gaps after picture lock.
- Final video matches duration, dimensions, and frame rate.
- Final output contains video and audio tracks.
- Motion audit reports no unexplained long freezes.
- Browser or render logs contain no unresolved errors.
- Contact sheet includes every scene midpoint, boundary, evidence close-up, and final frame.

## Final review sequence

1. Read the film once as a factual argument without watching it.
2. Listen once without looking and verify every voice and transition.
3. Inspect the contact sheet for repetition and template leakage.
4. Inspect all exact boundaries, subtitle changes, and evidence close-ups.
5. Watch the complete final film at normal speed with sound.
6. Reopen the delivered file and confirm metadata, duration, dimensions, frame rate, and audio.
